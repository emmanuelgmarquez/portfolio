The Events page is promoting events that already happened.

The "Events" link in the KevinTrudeau.com menu goes to kevintrudeauevents.com. Its "Upcoming Events" list still shows the July and September events, and the Family Reunion that just ended. The December 17 Chicago dinner, which went out by email on November 12, isn't on the page at all.

Why it costs money

Ready buyers hit a dead end. Someone who clicks "Events" wants to go to one. They see past dates and assume there's nothing coming up.

The next offer is invisible. The December 17 dinner is tied to buying a Money or Relationship Process, but site visitors never hear about it.

How I'd fix it

  1. Today: take down the past events and add the December 17 dinner with its offer and a clear button.
  2. Add a "tell me about the next event" signup so visitors between events still join the list.
  3. Keep it current: give every event an end date and check the page whenever a new event email goes out.
02

KevinTrudeau.com makes visitors download 46 MB of video.

The homepage has 48 video players. Every one is a full 1080p file. When I opened the page, it pulled down 46 MB of video within seconds, without me scrolling or pressing play. Only the top video plays on its own, and that effect should stay. But that one file alone is 15.7 MB. On a phone, that's a slow page and a big chunk of someone's data plan before they ever reach a "Buy" button.

For comparison, a typical web page is a few megabytes in total.

Why it costs money

Speed sells. Google found that 53% of mobile visitors leave a page that takes more than three seconds to load. Every visitor who leaves never sees the offers.

Paid traffic gets wasted. Ad clicks and email clicks land on this page. A slow first screen burns that spend.

How I'd fix it

  1. Keep the top video playing, same look. Re-export it as a compressed file (around 1–2 MB) with a smaller mobile version. Visitors see the same effect, just faster.
  2. Load the other videos only when someone presses play. The cover photos are already there, so the page looks the same.
  3. Test before and after with Google PageSpeed Insights and report the change in load time and clicks to the store.
